# Midi4Text on mesa3 — first-pass mapping

A complete chord assignment for all four Series on a 9-key-per-hand board, for review.
Generated by `midi4text-analysis/first_pass.py`.

## The board

Each hand: `a` (pinky), `o`/`s` (ring), `t`/`n` (middle), `e`/`i` (index), and two thumbs
written **`Sp`** and **`Bk`**, as in `BIT_NAMES`. **Dosh swaps the thumbs relative to
Taipo**, so `Bk` is the key that types Space and `Sp` the one that types Backspace.
Chords are written left-hand, hyphen, right-hand; thumb names are two letters, so `eiSp`
reads unambiguously as `e` + `i` + `Sp`.

    consonant group   a o s t n       Series 1 (onset) left, Series 4 (coda) right
    vowel group       e i Sp Bk       Series 2 left, Series 3 (vowel) right

This split follows Michela's own finger assignment: Series 1 never uses more than three
keys and lives on pinky, ring and middle, while the vowels sit on index and thumb.

## Midi4Text's central rule, preserved

Michela is phonetic and marks no inter-word space at all — it is implicit in each word's
abbreviation. Midi4Text is orthographic, so it must mark spaces, and it adopts the
"space" convention: **every vowel has a plain form and an *ending* form, the ending form
being the plain one plus a key, and using it closes the word and emits the space.**

That rule is why the space never costs a stroke, and it is what the data confirms without
a single exception across all 68,235 ending-form entries: ending form → whole word; plain
vowel → binds forward; empty Series 3 → binds backward.

The mapping keeps it exactly, with **`Bk` as the word-end marker** — and since `Bk` is the
thumb that types Space in Dosh, the mnemonic is exact: the space thumb adds the space.

(Phoenix marks boundaries on a similar principle. What lets Midi4Text's version stay
exceptionless is that the marker attaches to an orthographic vowel slot that is always
present, so there is no phonetic irregularity for it to trip over.)

## Design rules

1. **Dosh transfer.** `s`, `t` and `n` are the three commonest Midi4Text consonants *and*
   Dosh keys, so they keep their Dosh chords for free. Likewise `e` and `i` in the vowel
   group.
2. **The pinky as a voicing modifier.** Where a voiced/unvoiced pair exists, the rarer
   member is the commoner one plus the pinky: d = t + pinky, b = p + pinky, g = c + pinky,
   v = f + pinky, z = s + pinky, sh = ch + pinky.
3. **One vowel, one chord, either hand.** Series 2 keeps the Series 3 chord wherever it
   means the same vowel, so `e`, `i`, `o` and `u` read identically on both hands.

The pinky is not avoided — with only the lower pinky key present it is not a weak
position, so chords are scored purely by frequency times size.

## What it costs

| | keys/word | keys/stroke | strokes of 7+ keys |
|---|---|---|---|
| **first pass** | **7.69** | 4.20 | **12.0%** |
| frequency-greedy, no structure | 7.61 | 4.16 | — |
| Michela's own encoding | 8.36 | 4.56 | 22% |
| Dosh | 8.13 | 1.38/chord | — |

**The structure costs 1%** — 7.69 against the unconstrained 7.61 — and still beats
Michela's encoding by 8% and Dosh by 5%, while more than halving the proportion of very
large strokes.

## Known gaps

- The three rare coda-only shapes (final `h`, the `AE` tail, and the unused onset slot) are
  parked on four- and five-key chords. They are individually under 0.1% of slots, so this
  costs nothing measurable, but they deserve a deliberate home.
- Capitalisation, the number bar, and the punctuation and command chords have no assignment
  yet. On Michela these live in Series 4's spare patterns; here Series 4's 5-key group has
  one spare chord and the vowel groups have two each, which is not obviously enough.
- Nothing here has been tried by hand. The voicing-pair mnemonic in particular is a guess
  about what will feel memorable.
